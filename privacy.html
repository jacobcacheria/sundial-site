Sundial Privacy Policy

Effective: September 4, 2026

Sundial is a small app for staying close to the handful of people who matter most. You share a day with someone, and it fills up with notes, photos, drawings, voice, and songs. This page says exactly what Sundial collects, what it cannot read, and how to get rid of all of it. It is deliberately short, because there is not much to say.

The one-paragraph version

What you send is end-to-end encrypted on your device before it leaves it. Our servers only ever hold encrypted data, so we cannot read your messages, see your photos, or hear your voice notes. We never ask for your email address, phone number, contacts, or location. We do not show ads, and we do not sell anything about you to anybody, ever.

Who we are

Sundial is made by Jacob Cacheria. You can reach us any time at sundialskeeper@yahoo.com.

What you send, and why we cannot read it

Everything you put into an orbit is encrypted on your phone before it is uploaded, and it can only be opened by the phones of the people in that orbit. That covers your written beams, sunprints and sundogs, inklings, hums, moonflowers, golden hours, backdrops, and the captions on any of them.

Our servers, and the companies that run them, only ever store the encrypted version. Nobody at Sundial can open it. Neither can Google, whose infrastructure the encrypted data sits on.

One consequence is worth saying out loud: if you lose your device and your keychain, that history cannot be recovered. Not by you, and not by us. That is the trade for us not being able to read it.

What is not encrypted

Some information has to be readable by the server for the app to work at all. We would rather list it than let you assume otherwise:

  • The names and nicknames you type for yourself and for the people you beam with.
  • Which accounts share an orbit, and when that orbit was made.
  • For each thing you send: the time it was sent and what kind of thing it was (a beam, a sunprint, a hum, and so on). Not its contents.
  • Emoji reactions, and the storage path of an encrypted file.
  • Your notification token, so a push can reach your phone.
  • Usage counters and events, described below.

Your account

You sign in with Apple. Apple gives us an anonymous identifier for your account and nothing else. Sundial does not request or receive your email address, and there is no password to lose.

Usage information

Sundial records a small amount of information about how the app is used, so we can tell which parts work and which are broken. That means counters and event names such as "a beam was sent", "a sunprint failed to load", or "the almanac was opened", along with your account identifier and a nickname generated from it.

These records say that something happened, never what it was. The contents of anything you send never appear in them. There is no third-party analytics company involved, no advertising identifier, and no cross-app or cross-site tracking of any kind.

Camera, microphone, photos, and music

  • Camera is used only when you are taking a sunprint or a sundog.
  • Microphone is used only while you are recording a hum.
  • Photo library is used when you choose a backdrop, and when you save a sunprint back to your own roll.
  • Apple Music and Spotify are contacted only when you look for a song to send, or export a playlist. Those searches go to them under their own privacy policies.

Nothing from any of these is uploaded except the specific thing you chose to send, and that goes up encrypted like everything else.

Purchases

Sundial Gold is a one-time purchase handled entirely by Apple. Payment details never touch our servers and we never see them. We store only whether an account has unlocked it.

Where it is stored

Sundial runs on Google Firebase (Firestore, Cloud Storage, Cloud Functions, and Cloud Messaging), and uses Apple for sign-in, push notifications, and payment. Everything is encrypted in transit, and your content is additionally encrypted by your own phone before it gets there.

  • Apple: Apple Privacy Policy
  • Google Firebase: Firebase Privacy and Security

How long things last

A day's beams leave the main page at midnight and are kept in your almanac, which is private to the people in that orbit. If somebody leaves an orbit, the shared history is deleted within twenty-four hours. Deleting your account removes your account and its data.

Deleting everything

You can delete your account from inside the app, in Settings. It takes your account and its data with it and cannot be undone. If you would rather we did it, or you no longer have the app, email sundialskeeper@yahoo.com and we will.

Children

Sundial is not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect anything from them. If you believe a child has made an account, email us and we will remove it.

Changes

If this policy changes, the effective date at the top changes with it, and the new version is posted here.

Contact

Questions, deletion requests, or anything at all: sundialskeeper@yahoo.com.
